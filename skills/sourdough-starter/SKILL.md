# Sourdough starter

1. **What you need to know first:** how much starter they have, the flour (white, whole wheat, rye), its hydration
   (most people keep 100%: equal weights of flour and water), whether it lives on the counter or in the fridge, the
   room temperature, and when it was last fed. Use grams; give °C and °F.
2. **Ratios.** Written starter : flour : water by weight. 1:1:1 with 50 g starter is 50 g + 50 g + 50 g = 150 g. A
   bigger ratio (1:2:2, 1:5:5) is more food: it peaks later and suits longer gaps or warm rooms.
3. **Amounts for a target.** Divide the target by the sum of the parts, then multiply by each part. 200 g at 1:2:2:
   200 ÷ 5 = 40, so 40 g starter, 80 g flour, 80 g water. Add 10–20 g for what sticks to the jar.
4. **Hydration.** Water ÷ flour × 100. The starter's own flour and water count: 100 g of a 100% starter is 50 g flour
   and 50 g water. Moving a starter to 75%: feed it with water at three quarters of the flour weight for a few feedings
   and it settles near 75%. Using a runny starter in a recipe written for a stiff one: work out the flour and water in
   both and take the difference off the recipe's water. Example: a recipe wants 100 g of a 50% starter (about 67 g flour
   and 33 g water); 133 g of a 100% starter carries the same 67 g flour but 67 g water, so use 133 g and cut the
   dough's water by about 33 g.
5. **Timing by temperature** (a fed 1:1:1 starter, roughly):
   - around 18–20 °C (64–68 °F): slow, peak in 8–12 hours;
   - 21–24 °C (70–75 °F): 5–8 hours;
   - 25–27 °C (77–81 °F): 4–6 hours;
   - over about 28 °C (82 °F): fast and very sour; use bigger ratios or cooler water.
   At its peak it has at least doubled, is domed and full of bubbles; once the top sinks it's past peak (more sour, a
   little weaker). Senses beat the clock.
6. **Planning a bake backwards.** From the time they want to mix the dough, count back the hours to peak at their
   temperature. Example, dough at 09:00 Saturday, starter in the fridge, 200 g needed at about 22 °C: Thursday evening,
   a small 1:1:1 feed to wake it; Friday morning, keep 20 g and feed 1:2:2; Friday around 21:00, build the amount at
   1:5:5 (20 g + 100 g + 100 g) so it peaks by morning. Give the plan with real times and, if they want, a
   `set_reminder` for each feeding.
7. **Troubleshooting:**
   - grey liquid on top ("hooch"): it's hungry; pour it off for milder bread or stir it in for more sour, then feed;
   - a nail-polish or acetone smell: starving; feed more often or at a bigger ratio for two or three days;
   - not rising: too cold (a warmer spot, such as an oven with only the light on), too weak (feed twice a day at
     1:2:2 for three or four days), or chlorinated water (let tap water stand out or use filtered); a spoon of whole
     wheat or rye flour helps;
   - a new starter that went quiet on days 3–5: normal; keep feeding;
   - runny and flat: a bit more flour at the next feed;
   - mould (fuzzy spots) or pink or orange streaks: throw it all away and clean the jar; don't try to rescue it.
   The float test is only a hint, and unreliable with whole-grain starters.
8. **Going away.** Up to a few weeks: feed it thick (1:5:5), leave it an hour or two, then fridge it in a jar with
   room to grow; back home, two or three room-temperature feeds before baking. Months: spread a thin layer on baking
   paper, dry it fully, break it into flakes and store them sealed; revive over a few days with flour and water.
   Freezing is unreliable.
9. **Discard** makes good pancakes, crackers and flatbread.
10. **A feeding log,** if they want to learn their starter's rhythm and have the Library: `library_append` a row
    (date, time, ratio, room temperature, hours to peak).

## Done when
- Amounts are worked out in grams for their ratio and target, with the sum shown.
- The timeline fits their room temperature and the time they want to bake.
- A problem got a likely cause and a concrete fix, and mould means starting over.
- Feeding reminders were offered for a planned bake.
